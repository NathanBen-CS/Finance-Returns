FLAGS
  1. Reframing: Phase 2's persistence question compares CAR[-1,+1] with CAR[-1,+5] for the same filing (decay within one event). This task asks a different question: whether one filing's reaction predicts the same company's next filing's reaction (persistence across events).
  2. Pairs that share reaction days inflate persistence. 18,888 (4.63%) transitions have the same reaction day 0 and so the same CAR and the same reaction_category by construction; gaps of 1-2 trading days share part of the CAR window. Same reaction as previous: 30.26% over all transitions, 24.76% with gap >= 3.
  3. Once pairs that share CAR days are removed, little order effect is left. Same reaction as previous is 24.76% with gap >= 3; shuffling each company's reactions (which keeps its mix and removes order) gives 24.55%; independence from the company gives 21.70%. So of the 3.06 points above independence, 2.85 are matched by company mix alone (companies that often have Strong reactions keep having them, in any order). The shuffle covers all transitions, not only the gap >= 3 ones, so this split is approximate.
  4. Order: filings are sequenced by filing_date as asked, but reactions are measured from report_date. 6,572 transitions (1.61%) have the earlier-filed filing's reaction day later than the next filing's.
  5. lift uses curr's base rate among the transitions in each variant; among all filings the rates differ by at most 0.03 points.
  6. Robustness A changes which pairs exist, not just their number: 393,498 transitions (vs 407,540), and its gaps are longer (median 49 trading days vs 14), and it keeps 7,545 (1.92%) gap-0 pairs (vs 18,888 in the main table).
  7. Inherited from Phase 2: filings on the same company and trading day share their CAR; size and volatility are October 2026 snapshots; the universe has survivorship bias.
