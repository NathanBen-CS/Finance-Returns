STEP 2: WHY THESE FOUR K VALUES
  k=2 is a pure direction split: Up = car_minus1_plus1 > 0, Down = car_minus1_plus1 <= 0, defined from the CAR's sign and independent of the 5-class bucket.
  k=3 is sign with magnitude dropped: Negative (Strong Negative + Negative), Neutral, Positive (Positive + Strong Positive).
  k=4 has no clean collapse of the 5-class label. It is only an interpolation point on the elbow and silhouette curves, so no label is forced onto it.
  k=5 is reaction_category as stored, which meets the assignment's rule that one k must equal the number of existing classes.
  k=6 to 10 only extend the elbow curve for context and are not part of the comparisons.
  The assignment names k=3, 4, 5. k=2 is added on top of that, for the direction question.
  Up/Down counts: 200,655 (51.18%) Down, 191,383 (48.82%) Up. 3 rows have a stored CAR of exactly 0 and count as Down.
  3-way counts: 181,143 (46.21%) Negative, 38,837 (9.91%) Neutral, 172,058 (43.89%) Positive
  Up/Down is not a collapse of the 5 classes: the Neutral rows split 19,512 Down and 19,325 Up.

STEP 9: COMPARISON AND VERDICT
  k=2 vs Up/Down: not recovered. Purity 0.5170 is 0.51 points above always guessing Down, NMI 0.0010, ARI 0.0011. The clusters split companies by size (NMI with size_category 0.566, ARI 0.451), and Up share is 50.5% and 46.8%.
  k=3 vs Negative/Neutral/Positive: not recovered. Purity 0.4662 is 0.41 points above the largest class, NMI 0.0044, ARI 0.0006. Negative share is 42.9% to 51.6% and Positive 42.0% to 44.9% across clusters; Neutral runs 6.5% to 12.9%. NMI with size_category is 0.667.
  k=5 vs reaction_category: the highest alignment of the three and still weak. Purity 0.2860 is 1.64 points above the largest class, NMI 0.0153, ARI 0.0124. The small part it finds is magnitude: Strong share runs from 37.1% in the cluster with the largest companies to 69.3% in the cluster with the smallest, while Up share only runs 44.1% to 50.8%. NMI with Strong vs not is 0.0199 and with Up/Down 0.0010.
  The axis the clusters follow is company size and exchange, as in Task 1. NMI with size_category is 0.566, 0.667, 0.620 at k=2, 3, 5 and with exchange 0.234, 0.276, 0.462, against 0.0010, 0.0044, 0.0153 with the matching label. Task 1's PC1 is company size and its PC2 is mainly NMS vs NYQ. On the 94.1% of events with a Task 1 score, the clusters hold 64.4%, 81.1% and 87.3% of the variance of PC1, and at k=5 also 54.4% of PC2. They group companies, not filings: at k=5, 96.9% of companies sit wholly in one cluster, NMI with event_category is only 0.0128, and Financial/Earnings is the most common filing in all five clusters, so a name like "large-cap tech earnings filers" would claim more than the clusters show.
  Why they cannot do better: only 1.46% of the Up/Down variance lies between companies, and 0.94% would appear by chance. Direction changes from filing to filing within a company, and these clusters do not.
  Same test rows, 5-class label: tree 31.48%, Naive Bayes 29.17%, labelled clusters 28.48%, baseline 26.96%. NMI 0.0345, 0.0270, 0.0118; ARI 0.0367, 0.0262, 0.0213.
  Same test rows, 3-way label: tree 47.13%, Naive Bayes 40.91%, labelled clusters 46.63%, baseline 46.21%. The clusters have the higher NMI and ARI here (0.0038 and 0.0122, tree 0.0014 and 0.0040), but all of these are close to zero.
  Same test rows, Up/Down: k=2 behaves differently. Tree 51.61%, Naive Bayes 51.38%, labelled clusters 51.61%, baseline 51.22%. The clusters tie the tree because neither finds direction. Both lean on size: Up share runs from 44.6% at Small to 50.7% at Mega-cap, and the tree says Up for 11.7% of Small and 67.1% of Mega-cap rows. They agree on 67.0% of test rows, and where they differ the tree is right 50.0% of the time.
  Verdict: classification is the better approach for predicting reaction_category when a label is available, and that rests on the decision tree. On the 5-class label the tree gains 4.52 points over the baseline against 1.52 for labelled clusters, and it stays ahead on unseen companies (+4.26 vs +1.74) and on the later rows (+0.83 vs -1.23). The clusters also need those same labels to predict anything. Naive Bayes does not share this clearly: +2.21 vs +1.52 for labelled clusters on the Task 2 split, level on unseen companies (+1.78 vs +1.74) and on the later rows (-1.33 vs -1.23), and 5.30 points below the baseline on the 3-way label, where it predicts Neutral for 20.9% of rows against 9.9% in truth. The tree's win is about how large a reaction is, not which way it goes. On direction no approach is better and none is useful: the gain is under half a point on the Task 2 split, and all three fall below the baseline on the later rows. Clustering is better described as surfacing company structure (size and exchange) than reaction structure.

FLAGS
   1. The brief expected 61 encoded columns. These 8 features give 33, the same as Task 2: 2 ordinal + 2 log columns + 11 sector + 6 exchange + 7 event_category + 5 filing_day_of_week. 61 cannot come from this feature set; nothing was added to reach it.
   2. The size of each column matters here, which it did not in Task 2. Feature scale barely affects trees and Naive Bayes, but it drives k-means. With the requested encoding, size_category, log_market_cap and log_employees hold 50.5% of the total variance, and size_category alone holds 19.6% because it stays on a 1-4 scale. So size is what k-means mostly sees. Those columns also carry nearly one fact three times, as size_category is quartiles of market_cap. The encoding is kept as requested.
   3. The cluster boundaries depend on that scaling, but the finding does not. Z-scoring the ordinals changes the partition a lot at k=3 and k=5 (ARI with the main clusters 0.280 and 0.349), and removing the size columns moves the clusters onto exchange and event_category (at k=5, NMI 0.714 and 0.376). Across all these checks the largest ARI with the matching label is 0.0140. Describe the clusters as what this encoding produces, not as natural groups. Fitting the z-score on Task 2's training rows instead changes almost nothing (ARI with the main clusters 1.000 at k=5).
   4. Rows from one company repeat one position on five of the eight features, because sector, exchange, size_category, market_cap and employees are per-company values. Only event_category, filing_day_of_week and filing_timeliness can move a filing, and they carry 25.0% of the variance. Companies that file more count more.
   5. Snapshot limitation: size_category, market_cap and employees are single October 2026 values attached to events back to 2016-01-04, and sector and exchange are snapshots too, so cluster membership uses information from after most events.
   6. The only direction signal may be an artefact. Up share is 44.1% in the cluster with the smallest companies and 50.8% in the one with the largest. Companies that are small today may be small because their stock fell, and that cluster's mean CAR is +1.12% with a median of -0.97%, so skewed returns would also produce this. Without point-in-time market caps, this data cannot separate those from a real effect. Do not read it as a way to predict direction.
   7. Size stands in for the excluded volatility, and this accounts for most of the magnitude pattern. trailing_volatility_1y is not an input, but the k=5 clusters line up with its quartiles (NMI 0.165), with mean volatility running 0.34 to 2.00. The Strong share differs by 32.3 points between clusters overall but only 5.2 to 12.7 points inside one volatility tier. Inside a tier, on the 5-class test rows, the tree gains -1.02 to +2.98 points over that tier's largest class and labelled clusters -5.52 to -0.19. So excluding the column did not fully remove what it measures.
   8. Purity has a floor: it cannot fall far below the largest class's share, so read it against that share and not on its own. NMI and ARI are clearly above their shuffled values, but with 392,038 rows that shows little. The size of the effect is the point, and these rows are not independent: 36,211 (9.24%) share a company and trading day with another row, and always share its CAR.
   9. Labelling clusters by majority is a yardstick, not a fair test of clustering, which never tries to predict anything. It also predicts few classes: at k=5 only Positive, Strong Negative, Strong Positive, and at k=3 never Neutral. Several k=5 majorities are near ties.
  10. The random split flatters every approach. Training on the earliest 80% and testing from 2025-01-13, the 5-class baseline is 32.25%: tree 33.07%, Naive Bayes 30.91%, labelled clusters 31.02%. On Up/Down the baseline is 53.58%: tree 51.87%, Naive Bayes 49.63%, labelled clusters 51.62%. On companies not seen in training, 5-class: tree 31.31%, labelled clusters 28.79%, baseline 27.05%.
  11. filing_timeliness and filing_day_of_week can come after the reaction, as flagged in Task 2. This matters little here: their NMI with the clusters is at most 0.0049.
  12. k-means can settle in different local solutions at k=5. Over five other seeds SSE runs 1,338,966 to 1,339,122 (main 1,338,965) and ARI with the main clusters runs 0.818 to 0.999, so one seed finds a visibly different partition with a slightly worse SSE. ARI with the label stays at 0.0124 to 0.0130. No seed was chosen for its result.
  13. Silhouette uses a 20,000-row sample, not all rows. Over five other samples it moves by at most 0.0022, so k=4 and k=5 cannot be ranked. Mixing one-hot and numeric columns in one Euclidean distance also limits how much the score can mean.
  14. Inherited from Task 2: 3 rows with a stored CAR of exactly 0 count as Down, one-way price suspects can still affect labels (not cluster inputs), blank employees removed more small companies, and the universe has survivorship bias.
