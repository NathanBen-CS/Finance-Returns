STEP 7: COMPARISON
  F1: tree wins Strong Negative, Negative, Strong Positive; Naive Bayes wins Neutral, Positive.
  AUC: tree wins Strong Negative, Negative, Neutral, Positive, Strong Positive; Naive Bayes wins none.
  Side of zero, among rows where neither truth nor prediction is Neutral: tree 52.13%, Naive Bayes 52.11%; 48.71% of these true classes are up.
  Largest tree importances: log_market_cap 0.354, log_employees 0.153, event_category=Financial/Earnings 0.142, sector=Financial Services 0.102, sector=Real Estate 0.060, sector=Healthcare 0.028

  The tree wins overall: accuracy 31.48% vs 29.17%, macro F1 0.255 vs 0.233, macro AUC 0.627 vs 0.605. Both sit only a little above the 26.96% largest-class baseline and the 0.5 AUC of guessing.
  F1: tree wins Strong Negative, Negative, Strong Positive; Naive Bayes wins Neutral, Positive.
  AUC: tree wins Strong Negative, Negative, Neutral, Positive, Strong Positive; Naive Bayes wins none.
  Naive Bayes wins F1 on Neutral and Positive because it predicts them far more often (8.0% and 34.0% of test rows, against 0.2% and 13.3% for the tree), not because it ranks them better: the tree has the higher AUC on both. In return it almost never predicts Negative (0.4% of rows, recall 0.005).
  Why the tree does better: the effects combine. An earnings filing lifts the chance of a Strong reaction from 30.6% to 51.5% at Mega-cap companies but only from 66.8% to 69.8% at Small ones. A tree can split on that combination. Naive Bayes multiplies one independent effect per column, so it cannot. This reading is supported by what did not help Naive Bayes when tested (see the sensitivity table): giving it one size column instead of three, or discrete inputs instead of bell curves, barely changes its AUC.
  What both models learn is how large a reaction is, not which way it goes. The share of Strong reactions runs from 40.1% at Mega-cap to 67.9% at Small, and from 31.1% in Utilities to 65.2% in Healthcare. The side of zero is close to a coin flip: when both truth and prediction have a side, the tree is right 52.1% of the time and Naive Bayes 52.1%, and 51.3% of true non-Neutral rows are down. This is why each model's most common mistake on the two Strong classes is the opposite Strong class.

FLAGS
   1. Weak signal: the tree reaches 31.48% and Naive Bayes 29.17%, against 26.96% for always guessing Strong Negative. The features describe the company and the kind of filing, not the news itself, so this is expected.
   2. Snapshot limitation: size_category, market_cap and employees are not point-in-time. Each of the 3,703 companies has one value for all its events, taken in October 2026 and attached to events back to 2016-01-04. sector and exchange are snapshots too. Today's market_cap also reflects returns earned after the earlier events, so this is look-ahead.
   3. market_cap works as a company ID: 3,703 distinct values for 3,703 companies. An unlimited tree can use it to memorise a company's history. Depth 10 shows little of this: with no company in both train and test, tree accuracy moves from 31.48% to 31.31% and macro AUC from 0.6271 to 0.6186. The unlimited tree falls to 26.29% on unseen companies.
   4. Size partly stands in for the excluded volatility: across companies, Spearman(market_cap, trailing_volatility_1y) is -0.59. Across events, Spearman with |CAR[-1,+1]| is -0.26 for market_cap and +0.37 for volatility. No formula ties them together, but they are not independent. Removing all three size columns takes macro AUC from 0.6271 to 0.6130 for the tree and from 0.6048 to 0.5993 for Naive Bayes.
   5. size_category is pd.qcut of market_cap, so it holds no extra information, and Naive Bayes treats the three size columns as independent evidence. Both are kept as requested. Using market_cap as the only size column barely changes the scores (Naive Bayes macro AUC 0.6036, tree 0.6231).
   6. Timing: filing_timeliness and filing_day_of_week are known at filing, but the label's window is anchored on report_date, not filing_date. 99,737 (25.44%) of events were filed after CAR[-1,+1] had closed, including every Slightly Late and Late row. For those rows these two features are not known before the reaction. No formula links them to CAR, and removing both leaves the results almost unchanged (tree macro AUC 0.6283, Naive Bayes 0.6033).
   7. filing_timeliness uses calendar days from Phase 1: 86.9% of Slightly Late filings are within 4 business days, so on time under the SEC's rule. 1 row has a filing_date before report_date and the longest gap is 965 days; both are kept as filed.
   8. Same-day filings: 36,211 (9.24%) of rows share a company and trading day with another row, and always share its label because they share one CAR. A random split puts 7.65% of test rows beside a training row with the same answer. Keeping each company-day on one side gives tree accuracy 31.24% and macro AUC 0.6252.
   9. Naive Bayes is unstable on rare one-hot columns: 47 rows are Security Incident and 128 are PNK (one company). Their tiny variances differ by class and shift every row's score. Over five other random splits its accuracy runs 27.97% to 29.02% while AUC stays at 0.6039 to 0.6052. On the company-day split it scores 24.84%, below that split's 26.79% baseline. Without the two columns, accuracy is 29.77% on the main split and 29.48% to 29.86% on the five others. The columns are kept as requested, so compare the models on AUC more than on Naive Bayes accuracy. The tree moves far less: 31.29% to 31.55%.
  10. Neutral is the smallest class (9.91%) and the tree predicts it for 0.19% of test rows, so its precision and recall there rest on few predictions.
  11. Bad prices can remain: 41 kept events touch a one-way price suspect (raw/price_one_way_suspects.csv) and 854 have |CAR[-1,+1]| above 100%, some of them real. The label only records which bucket a CAR falls in, and removing the suspects leaves tree accuracy at 31.48%.
  12. Blank employees is not random: dropped rows are 7.5% of Small but 2.0% of Mega-cap events, so the rows used lean toward larger companies.
  13. Survivorship: the universe is today's SEC ticker list, so companies that disappeared since 2016 are absent, which is inherited from the Phase 2 dataset.
  14. A random split also ignores time. Training on the earliest 80% and testing from 2025-01-13 gives tree accuracy 33.07% and macro AUC 0.6189, against a baseline of 32.25% because the class mix shifts. That is only 0.83 percentage points above the baseline, and Naive Bayes falls below it at 30.91%, so the requested random split makes accuracy look better than it would be on future filings.
